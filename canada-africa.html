Connecting Canadian and African Commercial Intelligence

Canada and Africa are becoming increasingly connected through trade, investment, critical minerals, infrastructure, technology and other commercial sectors.

Canada's 2025 Africa Strategy explicitly aims to strengthen economic cooperation and support Canadian companies pursuing opportunities across African markets. The strategy also includes continued support for AfCFTA implementation and new mechanisms intended to strengthen Canada-Africa commercial engagement. (Global Affairs Canada)

For organisations, however, opportunity creates a practical challenge.

How do you assess a company, executive, partner or intermediary in a market where your existing information sources may be limited?

That is where Mbhidiya Intelligence operates.

Intelligence for an Increasingly Connected Africa

Cross-Border Opportunity Requires Cross-Border Intelligence

The African Continental Free Trade Area is intended to deepen intra-African trade, investment and regional value chains. Its Digital Trade Protocol also aims to create more harmonised rules and a more predictable environment for digital trade across participating African markets.

As organisations expand across borders, they increasingly need to make decisions about people and companies they have never worked with before.

A South African company may need a partner in Kenya. A Canadian investor may evaluate an opportunity in Côte d'Ivoire. A financial institution may onboard a company with operations across several jurisdictions. An investor may consider a company whose ownership, relationships and influence extend beyond one country.

This creates an intelligence problem.

The easier it becomes to connect markets, the more important it becomes to understand who operates within them.

Mbhidiya Intelligence helps clients reduce this information gap.
